I treated this as an image regression problem where a convolutional network learns solar geometry implicitly from the pixels rather than through any explicit formula. I used an image net pretrained residual network and fed the day of year as an extra sine and cosine input, because the date is known and makes the inverse problem better posed. I predicted latitude directly and represented longitude and hour as sine and cosine pairs so their wraparound is handled naturally, decoding them with an inverse tangent. I trained with a stable mean squared error under mixed precision, using only mild photometric augmentation so the shadow cues are preserved. Finally I calibrated each prediction by blending it with the training distribution at a weight chosen on held out data.

I treated this as an image regression problem where a convolutional network learns solar geometry implicitly from the pixels rather than through any explicit formula. I used an ImageNet pretrained residual network and fed the day of year as an extra sine and cosine input to the prediction head, because the date is known and is needed to make the inverse problem well posed. I predicted latitude directly and represented longitude and hour as sine and cosine pairs so their wraparound is handled naturally, decoding them back with an inverse tangent. I trained with a stable mean squared error on these targets under mixed precision, using only mild photometric augmentation so the shadow direction and length cues are preserved. Finally I calibrated each prediction by blending it with the training distribution at a weight chosen on held out data.
